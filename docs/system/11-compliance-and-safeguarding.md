# Compliance, privacy and safeguarding

This chapter is about the obligations that attach to a product whose users are children, and about the gap between the documents that claim those obligations are met and the code that would have to meet them. The English Hub is a UK controller under UK GDPR, an ICO Children's Code service, a self-declared **provider** of a high-risk AI system under the EU AI Act, and - for Qatari residents - a controller under the PDPPL. Four regimes, one small codebase, and a compliance library in `business-docs/` that is largely honest about its own gaps but is in places ahead of the code. Read the code first; the documents tell you what someone intended.

The single most useful thing to know before anything else: **the two-identity split described in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md) is the root cause of most of the defects in this chapter.** Supabase `auth.users` / `profiles` are keyed on a uuid, Prisma `User.id` is a cuid, and roughly 96% of accounts have no Prisma row at all ([`src/lib/consent.ts:7-24`](../../src/lib/consent.ts)). Every subject-rights surface has been rewritten at least once because it keyed on the wrong one, and at least one safeguarding surface has not been.

## 2. DSAR export: two routes, two stores, neither complete

There are **four** export-shaped endpoints. Each has a UI surface, but only two of
them hand the subject their data - the other two create a request row or serve a
teacher, which is a different thing entirely.

| Route                      | Reached from                                                    | What it returns                                                        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `POST /api/account/export` | `/account/data-export` page, and linked from the privacy notice | The real self-serve Art.15/20 download. Branches on identity           |
| `GET /api/dsar/export`     | `/dashboard/data-requests`, via `window.location.href`          | Same two-branch logic, IP-keyed rate limit, optional `format`/`dsarId` |
| `POST /api/privacy/export` | `/dashboard/privacy`                                            | Creates a `DataAccessRequest` row only. Returns no data                |
| `GET /api/school/export/*` | School portal                                                   | Teacher-facing class reports, not a subject right                      |

`POST /api/account/export` ([`route.ts:61-101`](../../src/app/api/account/export/route.ts)) is the one to understand, because its shape is the shape of the whole problem:

1. Resolve a Prisma `User` by `supabaseUserId`, falling back to email.
2. **If no Prisma row**, call `compileSupabaseNativeSubjectData()` ([`src/lib/data-retention.ts:369`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)) - which reads `auth.users`, `public.profiles` and the eleven tables in `SUPABASE_SUBJECT_TABLES` ([`data-retention.ts:167-184`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)): enrolments, module progress, assessment attempts, certificates, practice sessions, parental consents, human review requests, quiz responses, marking submissions, class students, school members.
3. **If there is a Prisma row**, call `compileUserData()` ([`src/lib/dsar.ts:57`](../../src/lib/dsar.ts)) - which reads profile, essays, AI feedback, consents, privacy settings, subscription, prior data requests and audit log.

**Neither branch reads both stores.** A person who has a Prisma row gets the Prisma export and none of their Supabase learning records; a person who does not gets the Supabase export and none of the Prisma essays. `CompiledUserData` ([`dsar.ts:8-23`](../../src/lib/dsar.ts)) has no field for the Supabase tables, and `SupabaseNativeSubjectData` has no field for essays. Because most accounts are Supabase-only, most exports take branch 2 and the omission is usually in the harmless direction - but it is not a designed outcome, and a person with both will silently receive half their data. The Supabase branch at least says what it covered and names `DPO_EMAIL` for anything missing ([`data-retention.ts:417-422`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)); the Prisma branch makes no such statement.

Also missing from both: `SafeguardingReport` rows (deliberate - see §4), `CookieConsent` rows, and the AI decision audit log, which lives in `AuditLog` under `action = 'ai_decision'` and therefore does appear in the Prisma branch's `auditLog` array but not in the Supabase branch.

**What was wrong before September 2026.** Both export routes keyed only on Prisma. For an account with no Prisma row they answered _"no account data found"_ - a refusal of an Art.15 request for data the company demonstrably held, to the ~96% of accounts that are Supabase-only. The docblock at [`account/export/route.ts:73-80`](../../src/app/api/account/export/route.ts) records this, and [`src/__tests__/dsar-supabase-native.test.ts`](../../src/__tests__/dsar-supabase-native.test.ts) pins the fix.

### The back-office request ledger, and the subject who cannot be written down

`POST /api/dsar` records a formal rights request as a `DataAccessRequest` row. `DataAccessRequest.userId` is a required foreign key to `User.id`, so for a Supabase-only account **the row cannot be created at all**. The route used to answer those subjects with 404 "User not found" - a refusal of a statutory right to roughly 96% of accounts.

`recordUnlinkedRequest()` ([`src/app/api/dsar/route.ts:26-120`](../../src/app/api/dsar/route.ts)) is the answer, and it is a good model for how this codebase now handles "I cannot do the normal thing": write a `DSAR_CREATED_UNLINKED` audit row with `userId: null` carrying the reference, type, Supabase id, email and deadline; email the DPO a hand-off marked `[Action required]`; then tell the subject **exactly which of those two things succeeded**. If neither did, it returns 503 and says so in words - "we could not record your request, so we are not going to tell you that we have". Copy that pattern rather than inventing a new one.

`checkDeadlines()` ([`src/lib/dsar.ts:264`](../../src/lib/dsar.ts)) is **dead code**. Nothing calls it, there is no cron for it in [`vercel.json`](../../vercel.json), and no alert fires when a DSAR passes its one-month `calculateDeadline()`. The clock is calculated and displayed; it is not watched. That holds for unlinked requests too: `GET /api/dsar` **does** list them back to the subject. When no Prisma row resolves it reads the `DSAR_CREATED_UNLINKED` audit rows keyed on the session uuid and maps each into the same response shape as a linked request - reference number from `resourceId`, type, `status: 'RECEIVED'`, the calculated one-month deadline, `daysRemaining` and `handledBy: DPO_EMAIL` ([`route.ts:347-377`](../../src/app/api/dsar/route.ts)). The branch exists, per its own comment, so that a submitted request does not look as though it had vanished. What is missing is enforcement, not visibility.

## 3. Art.17 erasure: honest now, and the button on `/account` is broken

`DELETE /api/account/delete` is the best-written route in this area and worth reading in full as a model for the codebase's current standards. It has exactly two lawful outcomes and refuses to invent a third ([`route.ts:8-39`](../../src/app/api/account/delete/route.ts)):

- **Branch A, account with a Prisma row.** Soft-delete (`accountStatus: 'DELETED'`, `deletedAt`), create or reuse a `DataAccessRequest` of type `ERASURE`, write an `AuditLog` row with `userId: null` so it outlives the subject, sign out, email a named purge date 30 days out. The retention cron does the hard delete.
- **Branch B, Supabase-native account.** There is no Prisma row to soft-delete and nowhere to record a grace period, so the identity is erased **immediately** by `eraseSupabaseIdentity()` ([`data-retention.ts:238`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)) and the response carries `erasedAt` and deliberately no purge date.

**What was wrong before September 2026 is the important part.** Branch B did not exist. The route answered Supabase-native accounts with `{ success: true, scheduledPurgeAt }` having deleted nothing at all - no auth user, no profile, no audit entry. Email, full name, date of birth, year group and guardian email survived indefinitely while the account holder was told in writing they would be erased on a named date. For most of the user base, "delete my account" was a lie with a date attached.

Three design decisions follow from that, and none should be softened:

- `eraseSupabaseIdentity()` **verifies by reading back** and throws `ErasureIncompleteError` if either row survives ([`data-retention.ts:186-199, 285-305`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)). The profiles row is deleted first and explicitly, so a failure of the `auth.users` admin call still leaves the profile PII gone - the compliant direction - rather than trusting a cascade that never ran.
- On failure the route returns **503 with the DPO address and an audit reference**, leaves the user signed in, and says in plain words that it will not claim a deletion it cannot prove ([`route.ts:318-333`](../../src/app/api/account/delete/route.ts)).
- `free_allowance_usage` has no foreign key to `User` by design, so nothing cascades and it is deleted explicitly in both erasure paths ([`data-retention.ts:306`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts), [`:955-967`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)). Those rows hold a salted sha256 of a client IP, which is still personal data.

**The trap.** [`src/app/account/page.tsx:161`](../../src/app/account/page.tsx) calls `fetch('/api/account/delete', { method: 'POST' })`. The route only exports `DELETE` ([`route.ts:206`](../../src/app/api/account/delete/route.ts)), so Next.js answers 405 with a non-JSON body, `res.json()` throws, and the user sees the generic `account.delete_error` string. It also sends no `confirm: 'DELETE'` body, so it would 400 even if the method matched. The erasure control on `/account` does not work. The working one is the dedicated page at [`src/app/account/delete/page.tsx:54-58`](../../src/app/account/delete/page.tsx), linked from `/dashboard/settings` and from the privacy notice. Fix the caller, not the route.

A second trap: every honest-refusal message routes the subject to `DPO_EMAIL`, hard-coded as `dpo@theenglishhub.app` ([`data-retention.ts:157`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)). The privacy page's own header comment says that mailbox is **not yet provisioned** and that live contact is `cj@upskillenergy.com` ([`src/app/legal/privacy/page.tsx:5-8`](../../src/app/legal/privacy/page.tsx)). An erasure that fails currently tells the subject to write to an address that may not receive. Verify the mailbox before trusting any of that copy.

`POST /api/privacy/delete-account` is a parallel, older soft-delete route reached from `/dashboard/privacy`, keyed on a different confirmation phrase (`'DELETE MY ACCOUNT'`) and with none of the branch-B handling. Treat it as legacy; do not add behaviour to it.

## 4. Safeguarding reporting: reports arrive, and nobody can triage them

This is the most serious open issue in the chapter, and it is two separate failures stacked.

**The submit path works and is careful.** `POST /api/safeguarding/report` ([`route.ts`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)) is public, accepts anonymous reports, rate limits at a deliberately generous 10 per IP per hour with a 429 body that gives the Childline number, writes a `SafeguardingReport` row and an `AuditLog` row, then emails the DSL. The alerting logic was hardened: **every** report alerts, not just HIGH/CRITICAL, because a child understating harm is exactly the case that must not sit unseen; `sendEmail()` returns `{success}` and never throws, so the old `.catch()` was dead code that made a failed alert look like a success; delivery now retries the primary inbox and falls back to a second, and writes a `SAFEGUARDING_ALERT_DELIVERY_FAILED` audit row if both fail ([`route.ts:185-239`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)).

**Failure one: `reporterId` is written with the wrong identity.** [`route.ts:155`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts) sets `reporterId: sessionUserId`, where `sessionUserId` is the Supabase auth uuid. `SafeguardingReport.reporterId` is a foreign key to `User.id`, a cuid ([`prisma/schema.prisma:448, 457`](../../prisma/schema.prisma)). The same line in the `AuditLog` write at [`route.ts:169`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts) has the same problem. If the foreign-key constraint exists in the live database, a **signed-in** child submitting a report gets a P2003 into the route's catch and a 500 that says "Failed to submit report. Please try again or call Childline"; an anonymous report, with `reporterId` null, succeeds. I could not verify whether the constraint is present in production without database access - do that with `information_schema` (see [`scripts/check-schema-drift.mjs`](../../scripts/check-schema-drift.mjs) and the baselining warning in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)) before concluding either way. Either the constraint exists and signed-in reporting is broken, or it does not and the column holds unusable values. Both matter, because `checkSafeguardingRetention()` ([`data-retention.ts:1059`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)) queries `where: { reporterId: userId }` with the **cuid**, so it will never match a report filed by a signed-in child, and the "retain until 25 or 7 years" hold that is meant to stop `hardDeleteUser()` deleting safeguarding records ([`data-retention.ts:871, 912-916`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)) will not engage. There is no test file for safeguarding anywhere in `src/__tests__/`.

**Failure two: nobody can action a report.** The `GET` list handler's identity lookup was fixed during this documentation pass - it now resolves through `tryPrismaUserId()` and logs a loud `SAFEGUARDING_QUEUE_UNREACHABLE` diagnostic when no active ADMIN exists ([`route.ts:296-322`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)). The `PATCH` handler at [`src/app/api/safeguarding/report/[id]/route.ts:41-44`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/[id]/route.ts) **still** does `prisma.user.findUnique({ where: { id: sessionUserId } })` with the Supabase uuid, so assignment, status changes and case notes return 403 to everybody. Fix it the same way.

Then the question the task asks: **does any user who could action a report actually exist?**

- Authorisation requires a Prisma `User` row with `role: 'ADMIN'`, and nothing else ([`route.ts:281`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts), [`[id]/route.ts:46`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/[id]/route.ts) - both select only `{ role: true }`). `accountStatus: 'ACTIVE'` is checked only by the `SAFEGUARDING_QUEUE_UNREACHABLE` diagnostic ([`:286-287`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)) and the assignee dropdown ([`:327`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)), not by the gate itself. Do not add it as a precondition when granting ADMIN to a DSL.
- `Role` defaults to `STUDENT` ([`schema.prisma:175`](../../prisma/schema.prisma)).
- The identity projection **cannot** produce an ADMIN: `mapRole()` maps only `teacher` and `parent` and defaults everything else to `STUDENT`, with the explicit comment "ADMIN and REVIEWER are never assignable" ([`src/lib/identity/projection.ts:111-118`](../../src/lib/identity/projection.ts)).
- The only code that writes `role: 'ADMIN'` is [`scripts/seed.ts:29`](../../scripts/seed.ts), a development seed that `CLAUDE.md` warns would put a published password into the live database, and `PATCH /api/admin/users/[id]` which can set the role but is itself gated on `verifyAdmin()`.
- There is **no admin safeguarding dashboard page**. `src/app/admin/` contains fifteen surfaces and none of them is safeguarding. The alert email tells the recipient to "Log in to the admin safeguarding dashboard to manage this report" ([`route.ts:96`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)). That dashboard does not exist.

So unless someone has hand-set `role = 'ADMIN'` on a Prisma row in production - which I cannot check from here, and which is the one thing that would change this answer - **no user can list, assign, or close a safeguarding report.** The queue is write-only. Reports do reach a human by email, which is what actually protects children today, but nothing is tracked, assigned or closed, and the 24-hour acknowledgement and 24/48-hour/7-day escalation timescales published at [`src/app/safeguarding/page.tsx:183, 386-407`](../../src/app/safeguarding/page.tsx) rest entirely on one person reading an inbox.

Note also that every other admin surface authorises on **email** via `verifyAdmin()` ([`src/lib/admin-auth.ts`](../../src/lib/admin-auth.ts), `ADMIN_EMAILS` / `SITE_ADMIN_EMAILS`), not on the Prisma role. Switching safeguarding to that pattern would make the queue openable in one line - but it widens access to child safeguarding disclosures to everyone on an env-var list, and that is an owner decision, not a tidy-up. The route's own comment says exactly this.

Where the alerts actually go: `DSL_EMAIL` and `DSL_FALLBACK_EMAIL` both default to `cj@upskillenergy.com` ([`route.ts:17-18`](../../src/app/api/safeguarding/report/route.ts)), deliberately, because `safeguarding@theenglishhub.app` - the address published on [`/safeguarding`](../../src/app/safeguarding/page.tsx) and [`/legal/complaints`](../../src/app/legal/complaints/page.tsx) - is not provisioned. Set `DSL_EMAIL` to the real DSL inbox in production. The public page names **Lauren Johnson** as the sole DSL with no deputy, a decision recorded inline at [`page.tsx:229-243`](../../src/app/safeguarding/page.tsx); [`src/app/legal/safeguarding/content.ts:48`](../../src/app/legal/safeguarding/content.ts) still says "DSL and Deputy DSL" and contradicts it.

## 5. Retention

Everything happens in one nightly cron, `GET /api/cron/data-retention` at 04:00 UTC ([`vercel.json`](../../vercel.json)), calling `cleanupExpiredData()` ([`data-retention.ts:449`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)). A second cron, `dormancy-check` at 03:30, runs `processChildDormancy()` ([`src/app/api/cron/dormancy-check/route.ts:70`](../../src/app/api/cron/dormancy-check/route.ts)).

**The child path therefore runs twice a day, not once.** `cleanupExpiredData()` calls `processChildDormancy(prisma)` itself, as step 1b ([`data-retention.ts:506`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)), so the 03:30 cron and the 04:00 cron both execute the full cycle - hard-delete children past the 30-day grace period, then warn the newly dormant. It is idempotent by construction (deletions run before warnings, and both queries re-filter on current state), so the second pass is usually a no-op, but it is a duplicated hard-delete path across two schedules half an hour apart and neither caller knows about the other. Anyone changing dormancy behaviour must account for both entry points.

The child dormancy path is stricter than the adult path by design and is the clearest piece of Children's Code implementation in the repo: 12 months rather than 24, deletions run before warnings so one cycle never both warns and deletes the same account, and an audit row for every action tagged `"Children's Code Standard 8 - Data Minimisation"` ([`dormancy.ts:62-90`](../../src/lib/privacy/dormancy.ts)).

**One gap in it matters, and the code reads as though it were closed.** The warning email is _addressed_ to a parent - the subject line is "Your child's English Hub account will be deleted due to inactivity" - but it is **sent only to the child's own address**. `findDormantChildren()` hard-codes `parentEmail: null` for every candidate ([`dormancy.ts:126`](../../src/lib/privacy/dormancy.ts)), under a `TODO(Phase-7)` saying so plainly, because `User` has no guardian-email column. `sendDormancyWarning()` sends unconditionally to `user.email` ([`:187`](../../src/lib/privacy/dormancy.ts)) and then guards the guardian send on `if (user.parentEmail)` ([`:190-191`](../../src/lib/privacy/dormancy.ts)) - a branch that is dead for every caller. The audit row records `parentNotified: !!user.parentEmail` ([`:208`](../../src/lib/privacy/dormancy.ts)), which is therefore always `false`. **No guardian is notified before a child account is deleted.** Do not answer an ICO question about who was told from the function's docblock, which says the email goes to the parent and the child both.

Two things `hardDeleteUser()` does that are easy to undo by accident ([`data-retention.ts:906-925`](../../src/lib/data-retention.ts)): step 9 **nullifies** `AuditLog.userId` rather than deleting the rows, so the erasure record outlives the subject, and step 10a explicitly deletes `CookieConsent` rows because that model has no cascading relation and would otherwise orphan with a live `userId`, keeping `ipHash` and user agent past the erasure. Both are deliberate.

The **breach register is not durable.** [`src/lib/breach.ts:84`](../../src/lib/breach.ts) stores records in `const breachLog: BreachRecord[] = []` - a module-level array, marked "replace with database in production", in serverless functions. A breach logged through `POST /api/breach` survives until the instance recycles and is invisible to every other instance. The ICO 72-hour deadline arithmetic, the NCGAA notification generator and the pre-filled ICO form are all correct and all operate on a store that loses its contents. Do not report the breach process as operational.

## 6. Children's Code and PDPPL: what the signup form really persists

The age gate itself is sound and is enforced in more than one place. Under-13s cannot self-sign-up and are routed to the parent-linked flow; 13-15 must supply a guardian email; parent accounts must be 18+ ([`src/app/auth/register/page.tsx:155-175`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)). `src/lib/identity/age.ts` is the shared source for `PARENTAL_CONSENT_AGE = 16` and `MINOR_AGE = 18`, and the guardian-consent loop in [`src/lib/parental-consent.ts`](../../src/lib/parental-consent.ts) is documented with unusual care - read its header before touching it, particularly the decision to write only `parental_consents` and never `consent_tokens`, and the durable `last_sent_at` / `send_count` throttle that stops a guardian's inbox being used as a relay when the rate limiter degrades.

But four things in and around registration are not what the surrounding comments imply.

**`applyChildDefaults()` is never called.** It exists at [`src/lib/privacy/apply-child-defaults.ts:33`](../../src/lib/privacy/apply-child-defaults.ts) and swallows its own errors by design. The only reference to it is a commented-out line in [`src/app/api/auth/register/route.ts:254-263`](../../src/app/api/auth/register/route.ts) whose TODO claims "The helper does not yet exist in that module" - that TODO is itself stale, the helper does exist. What actually applies the defaults is the **client**: the register page calls `getChildProfileDefaults()` and spreads it into the `profiles` upsert ([`register/page.tsx:274, 290`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)). That upsert is explicitly non-blocking - if it fails, registration proceeds and the error is only logged ([`register/page.tsx:317-330`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)).

**The high-privacy defaults apply at a narrower age than the Code requires.** `CHILD_DEFAULT_SETTINGS` documents the under-18 boundary at length and `isMinorAge()` implements it, but the register page gates the write on `isChildUser`, defined as **under 16** ([`register/page.tsx:271, 274`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)). A 16- or 17-year-old is flagged `is_minor: true` but receives the adult default block, which sets `streaks_enabled: true`, `personalised_recommendations: true`, `streak_notifications: true` and `nudge_notifications: true` ([`register/page.tsx:290-296`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)) - the exact mechanics `child-defaults.ts` says Standard 6 requires to be off. Six of the eight flags in `CHILD_DEFAULT_SETTINGS` do map to `profiles` columns, through `getChildProfileDefaults()` ([`child-defaults.ts:114-126`](../../src/lib/privacy/child-defaults.ts)), which returns those six plus a hard-coded `streaks_enabled: false`. Only `publicLeaderboards` and `geolocation` have no column and are enforced, if at all, in application code - the file's own note at [`:107-112`](../../src/lib/privacy/child-defaults.ts) says exactly that. The gap is the age band the write is gated on, not the coverage of the mapping.

**The PDPPL Art.17 consent is collected and then probably dropped.** The form writes `country`, `data_transfer_consent_qa` and `data_transfer_consent_qa_at` into `profiles` ([`register/page.tsx:307-310`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)). **No migration in `supabase/migrations/` creates any of those three columns.** If they do not exist, PostgREST rejects the whole upsert - not just those fields - and the failure is non-blocking, so nothing at all is written to `profiles` for that account. I could not determine from the repo whether the columns were added by hand in the Supabase dashboard; check `information_schema.columns` before relying on either answer, and note that `check-schema-drift.mjs` will not flag them because it only knows about columns some migration claims to create. Meanwhile `buildConsentRecords()` ([`src/lib/auth.ts:238`](../../src/lib/auth.ts)), which is the function that would push an `INTERNATIONAL_DATA_TRANSFER` record into the Prisma consent ledger, is **dead code with no callers**, and its consent-type strings (`TERMS_AND_CONDITIONS`, `PRIVACY_POLICY`, `AI_ESSAY_ANALYSIS`, `INTERNATIONAL_DATA_TRANSFER`, `PARENTAL_AWARENESS`) do not exist in the Prisma `ConsentType` enum ([`schema.prisma:32-41`](../../prisma/schema.prisma)), so it would throw if it were wired up. `getDefaultPrivacySettings()` at [`auth.ts:303`](../../src/lib/auth.ts) is dead in the same way. `POST /api/auth/register` writes no `Consent` rows at all; the only writers are `recordConsent()` / `withdrawConsent()` in [`src/lib/consent.ts:138, 191`](../../src/lib/consent.ts), and their only caller anywhere in `src/` is `/api/consent` (POST at [`route.ts:141`](../../src/app/api/consent/route.ts), DELETE at [`:207`](../../src/app/api/consent/route.ts)). The AI gate only **reads** the ledger - `checkMinorAIConsent()` ([`src/lib/consent-check.ts:219`](../../src/lib/consent-check.ts)) goes through `hasConsent()` and writes nothing. If you are asking what consent the ledger holds and what could have put it there, the answer is one route.

**And the country the subject declared never reaches Prisma.** After the Supabase signup the register page fires a non-blocking `POST /api/auth/register` to create the Prisma `User` projection, with `country: 'GB'` hard-coded into the body regardless of what the user selected ([`register/page.tsx:388`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)). The route stores it verbatim as `User.country` ([`api/auth/register/route.ts:239`](../../src/app/api/auth/register/route.ts)). A Qatari resident who ticked the Article 17 box therefore has a Prisma row saying they live in the United Kingdom. Only the `profiles` upsert carries the real value ([`register/page.tsx:307`](../../src/app/auth/register/page.tsx)), and that is the upsert this section has just established may be failing wholesale. Any future control that segments by residence - PDPPL transfer consent, Qatar content rules, data-residency routing - must not read `User.country`.

The consequence: `/legal/privacy-qatar` §6 tells Qatari users their explicit Article 17 consent is "captured at sign-up", and the ledger that would evidence it is empty for that consent type.

## 7. Sub-processors: the register is right and the pages are not wired to it

[`src/config/subprocessors.ts`](../../src/config/subprocessors.ts) is the reconciled, code-verified union of every processor named anywhere on the site, and it is the answer to "which list is authoritative". It exists because `/data-processing`, `/legal/privacy` and the Qatar notices each enumerated a **different** set, and a school buyer asking which one governed had no good answer.

Two honest `unconfirmed` entries carry real weight:

- **Anthropic** ([`:189`](../../src/config/subprocessors.ts)) is the only LLM provider the product calls - verified by dependency and grep, no OpenAI SDK, no `api.openai.com`, no GPT model string. Its `dpaStatus` and `zeroRetention` are both `'unconfirmed'`, because no-training and retention limits are governed by the **commercial contract**, not by any request flag - the installed SDK exposes no such option. This is checklist item 4 in [`business-docs/compliance/eu-ai-act/human-action-checklist.md`](../../business-docs/compliance/eu-ai-act/human-action-checklist.md) and is unclosed.
- **The transactional email provider** is `'unconfirmed'` because neither `resend` nor `postmark` is in `package.json`; the RoPA names Postmark and this register historically named Resend. The code imports `sendViaResend` from [`src/lib/email/resend.ts`](../../src/lib/email/resend.ts), which suggests Resend over an HTTP call, but the register deliberately does not upgrade a status on an inference.

**The register is not wired into the published pages.** `LIVE_SUBPROCESSORS` has no consumers outside the module; `/data-processing`, `/legal/privacy` §4, `/legal/ai-transparency` §6 and the Qatar notices still hand-maintain their own lists. Rewriting them is a change to a contractual data-protection disclosure and needs founder/DPO sign-off - that is why it has not been done, not an oversight. `SUBPROCESSORS_NEEDING_CONFIRMATION` ([`:302`](../../src/config/subprocessors.ts)) is the exact list counsel must close first.

Two technical controls sit behind these pages and are easy to miss:

**Row-level security is on, with zero policies, on purpose.** [`supabase/migrations/20260506_enable_rls_on_prisma_tables.sql`](../../supabase/migrations/20260506_enable_rls_on_prisma_tables.sql) enables RLS on eighteen Prisma-managed public tables, including `SafeguardingReport` and `AuditLog`, and adds only a `service_role_full_access` policy. RLS-on with no policy is deny-all for `anon` and `authenticated` via PostgREST, which closes the public-internet hole Supabase's scanner reported; the application is unaffected because it reaches those tables through Prisma with the service-role key. The service-role policy is strictly redundant and exists to document intent. **Consequence:** `.env.local` is production and carries the service-role key, so RLS protects you from the internet, not from anything you run locally.

**Cookie consent hashes the IP and keeps it short.** `POST /api/consent/cookie` stores `ipHash` as an HMAC-SHA256 truncated to 16 hex characters, plus a 512-character user agent ([`route.ts:48, 135-136`](../../src/app/api/consent/cookie/route.ts)). `CookieConsent` has no cascading relation to `User`, which is why `hardDeleteUser()` deletes it explicitly (§5). The same salted-hash reasoning governs the rate-limit counters and the `free_allowance_usage` IP rows: a digest of an IP with a **known** salt is reversible in minutes across the IPv4 space and would remain personal data, so the salt is secret and the retention is short.

**The AI decision log is hash-first by design.** [`src/lib/ai-audit-log.ts`](../../src/lib/ai-audit-log.ts) satisfies EU AI Act Art.12/19 record-keeping without building a standing corpus of children's writing: it stores a SHA-256 of the learner input plus its length, never the text, unless `AI_AUDIT_STORE_RAW_INPUT` is explicitly turned on. Rows are written into `AuditLog` with `action = 'ai_decision'` ([`:96, 424`](../../src/lib/ai-audit-log.ts)) - no new table. When identity cannot be resolved the row is still written with `userId: null` and a **hash** of the Supabase id rather than the id itself, because erasure nullifies `AuditLog.userId` and a raw uuid left in `details` would survive that erasure as a pointer to the erased person. This file was a complete no-op until the identity fix: every insert failed the foreign-key constraint and the catch swallowed it, which is why two model outages ran undiagnosed.

## 10. The document layer: what is in it and how to read it

`business-docs/compliance/` is about sixty files. It is not stale in the way the repo root is - most of it was written in May 2026 and revised in September - but it describes an intended control set, and the discipline for reading it is set out below.

| Folder              | Contents                                                                                  | How to treat it                                                                   |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `controls/`         | One file: `rate-limiting-control-status.md`                                               | **Canonical.** The pattern the rest should follow. See below                      |
| `eu-ai-act/`        | Docs 00-17 plus `human-action-checklist.md`                                               | Substantive and self-critical. The index's status line is wrong (§1)              |
| `childrens-code/`   | `01-assessment/`, `02-policies/` (9 policies), `03-dpias/` (4), `04-evidence/`, `05-ico/` | `dpia-ai-features-v1.md` asserts controls that do not exist; doc 15 reconciles it |
| `cyber-essentials/` | Readiness, 14 policies, IASME SAQ draft                                                   | Aspirational. Nothing here is certified                                           |
| `qatar-pdppl/`      | Art.16 permit, breach response, PDMS                                                      | Cross-check against §6 before quoting                                             |
| `rfc/`              | `ropa-v1.md`, parental-consent design, Qatar content classifier design                    | The RoPA names Postmark; the code suggests Resend (§7)                            |

The `human-action-checklist.md` in the AI Act pack is the most operationally useful file in the whole library: fourteen numbered acts that only a human can perform, each with a legal basis and the inputs already prepared. Items 1, 2 and 9 - confirm the EU-market trigger, get counsel sign-off on classification and residual risk, and name an internal AI compliance owner - unblock everything else and are all still open. Item 4 is the Anthropic DPA and ZDR confirmation that §7 turns on. Item 12 is the formal withdrawal of the v1.0 DPIA.

### The canonical-document convention, and why not to tidy

[`business-docs/compliance/controls/rate-limiting-control-status.md`](../../business-docs/compliance/controls/rate-limiting-control-status.md) declares itself **canonical** and, as of 18 September 2026, says the control is **not yet enforced in production**. This is a convention, not a one-off, and it is the most valuable pattern in the compliance library. Understand it before editing anything under `business-docs/compliance/`.

The convention: one file owns one control's factual status. Every other document **cites** that file rather than restating the control. When the status changes, one file changes, and the downstream documents are re-checked afterwards - never in anticipation.

The current state is subtle and it is the point. The Postgres backend for rate limiting is **written and tested** ([`src/lib/rate-limit.ts`](../../src/lib/rate-limit.ts), `getRateLimitHealth()` at `:466`, `describeRateLimitHealth()` at `:516`), and the migration exists ([`supabase/migrations/20260918_rate_limit_counter.sql`](../../supabase/migrations/20260918_rate_limit_counter.sql)). Neither fact makes the control enforced. Two things must both be true in production - the change is deployed, **and** the migration has been applied - and order matters: if the code ships before the migration lands, every limited request issues a failing insert and fails open, and the control is exactly as absent as it was the day before. That is why the health payload reports `enforcement: 'unproven'` until a decision has actually come back enforced, and why the document instructs you to read that line rather than the document.

So the status line still reads NOT ENFORCED, and the **eighteen** documents corrected on 17 September - listed in §9 of that file, and all eighteen verified present on disk - are therefore **still correct and must not be edited**. An earlier draft of that section said they were now out of date. It was wrong, and wrong in the dangerous direction: acting on it would have left eighteen compliance documents, including the EU AI Act Annex IV technical documentation, the Art.10 data-governance file, two Cyber Essentials policies and the RoPA, asserting an enforced control on the strength of code sitting in a working tree. A regulator reads those as statements of fact. Tidying them ahead of verification converts a known gap into a false attestation, which is a materially worse position than the gap.

The route out is §6 of that file, and only §6: run `getRateLimitHealth()` against production, confirm `backend: 'postgres'`, `enforcement: 'cross-instance'`, `databaseStatus: 'ready'`, confirm the Sentry issue "Rate limiting is not enforced (no shared backend)" has stopped recurring, **then** change the status line, **then** re-check the eighteen. `getRateLimitHealth()` is not yet wired into `/api/health` - doing so is the one-line change that would make this checkable without a deploy. Note that this migration sorts after `BASELINE_CUTOFF = '20260530'` ([`scripts/apply-migrations.mjs:42`](../../scripts/apply-migrations.mjs)), so it will genuinely execute rather than be baselined - the usual trap does not apply here.

The same discipline runs through the AI Act pack. [`business-docs/compliance/eu-ai-act/00-README-index.md:26-30`](../../business-docs/compliance/eu-ai-act/00-README-index.md) states plainly that the founder-signed v1.0 DPIA asserts technical controls that **do not exist in the codebase**, and that doc 15 exists to reconcile them. Item 12 of the human-action checklist is the formal withdrawal of that DPIA. Until that happens, a signed document in this repository claims controls the code does not have - treat `dpia-ai-features-v1.md` as superseded in substance and not yet in form.

## Correction, 18 September 2026

Three things this chapter suspected are now confirmed and two are fixed.

1. **Section 6 was right:** the Children's Code high-privacy defaults were never persisted for any account, because the signup profile write never succeeded (see chapter 05, correction of the same date). The trigger now computes and stores them for everyone under 18 at signup. Existing accounts have no stored date of birth and keep the adult defaults until a date of birth is supplied at the point of use; `PrivacySettings.marketingEnabled` (Prisma, default false) still governs digests independently.
2. **Qatar Article 17 consent was not captured at all**, not merely unevidenced: the columns did not exist. They exist now and the trigger stores `country` and, for QA, the consent flag and timestamp. Until real signups carry it, `/legal/privacy-qatar` §6 still overstates what is held.
3. **The safeguarding alert path failed at three layers** (section 4 and section 9): the route wrote a Supabase uuid into two Prisma foreign keys that exist in production (so a signed-in report would have failed at insert), all three delivery attempts used the unconfigured nodemailer transport, and the "fallback inbox" was the same address. Zero reports have ever been submitted, so nothing was lost. The route now resolves the Prisma id (null keeps a report anonymous rather than failing), sends the alert through Resend when `SMTP_HOST` is unset, and only tries the fallback when it differs. `DSL_EMAIL` is still unset in production, so alerts default to the founder's mailbox; naming the DSL inbox remains an owner action.
